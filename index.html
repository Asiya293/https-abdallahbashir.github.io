Where AI gets anatomy wrong

How I spot subtle errors in AI answers about the human body.

AI models are good at the textbook answer. In anatomy, the danger is the answer that is correct but incomplete. A response can sound confident, use the right terms, and still leave out the detail that matters clinically.

Example 1: Brachialis innervation

Ask a model what supplies brachialis and it will usually say the musculocutaneous nerve (C5-C6). That is the standard answer, but it is incomplete. The lateral part of the muscle commonly receives a branch from the radial nerve. A complete answer notes this dual supply, and explains why elbow flexion is often weakened but not lost after a musculocutaneous nerve injury.

Example 2: Scaphoid blood supply

A model may say only that the scaphoid is supplied by the radial artery. The missing detail is the direction of flow. Branches enter the bone mainly near its distal part, so blood reaches the proximal pole in a retrograde way. This is why a fracture through the waist or proximal pole can cut off the blood supply and lead to avascular necrosis.

How I check AI answers

First, I ask whether the answer matches what the clinical picture would look like. If an answer implies a muscle has a single nerve, I ask what an injury to that nerve would cause and whether that fits real presentations. Second, I look for what is missing, such as variations, laterality and root levels. Third, I verify against a standard anatomy text before I accept anything.

Models tend to repeat the simplest rule in their training data. A trained reviewer adds the exceptions, the clinical relevance and the reasoning, and that is the work I do.
